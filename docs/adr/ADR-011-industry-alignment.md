## Context and Problem Statement

- Rule R16 asks each project to list what practitioners expect, with a primary source, and how the project meets it or why not.
- CHN-1 was designed from retention practice, but the mapping was spread across ADR-001, ADR-004 and ADR-006 and never written down in one place.
- **Question:** does CHN-1 deliver what a subscription retention team expects, and where does it fall short?

## Decision Drivers

- Primary sources only (R6): peer-reviewed journals, a practitioner journal, the regulation itself.
- Each expectation maps to a file, an ADR or an honest gap.

## Expectations and how CHN-1 meets them

| # | Practitioners expect | Primary source | How CHN-1 meets it (or why not) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A fixed churn definition and labels that arrive late | WSDM Cup 2018 task definition | Churner = no new valid subscription within 30 days after expiry; out-of-time tests by expiry cohort (ADR-001, ADR-002) |
| 2 | Model accuracy matters because it changes campaign profit | Neslin et al. 2006, *JMR* | Models judged by churners reached and profit at a call budget, not only by AUC (ADR-004) |
| 3 | Select customers by value, not risk alone | Venkatesan & Kumar 2004, *J. Marketing*; Gupta et al. 2006, *J. Service Research* | Call list ordered by churn chance × member value (12 months of the current plan) (ADR-004) |
| 4 | Evaluate churn models by profit | Verbeke et al. 2012, *EJOR* | Profit curves and profit-based metrics in the appendix; 9 cost scenarios (ADR-004) |
| 5 | The riskiest customers may not respond to an offer | Ascarza 2018, *JMR* | **Gap:** no treatment data, so no uplift model; stated on the page; a randomised call test is the next step |
| 6 | Retention economics justify proactive outreach | Reichheld & Sasser 1990, *HBR* | Save rate and call cost are explicit inputs to every what-if (1 in 5 saved, 20 per call by default) |
| 7 | Profiling with a human decision; data minimisation | GDPR Art. 22 and Art. 5(1)(c) | Agents decide whom to call (no automated decision with legal effect); notes never quote age, gender or city; no member data published (ADR-006, ADR-010) |
| 8 | Deliverables the team can act on | Common retention workflow (sources 2–5) | A ranked call list with reasons and one suggested action per member; API and MCP tools for a CRM or assistant (ADR-005, ADR-006) |
| 9 | A monthly cadence with monitoring | Population stability index practice (Siddiqi, ADR-009) | Monthly Databricks job, drift check per input, dashboard as code (ADR-008, ADR-009) |

## Consequences

- **Positive:** one place shows a retention lead what the system answers and where it stops.
- **Negative:** expectation 5 (who responds to a call) stays open until a randomised call test exists.
