P4 · What is the call list worth? · try your own assumptions
Probability × valueProbabilityLambdaMART

Save rate and cost per call are not in the data, so they are assumptions; prices are in the dataset's units (plans cost 99–149 a month). Member value = 12 months of the current plan. The 9 tested scenarios are in the appendix.

What P4 showed
  1. Calling the top 10% protects about 12.4M a month after call costs, covering 66% of the 107.9M at risk.
  2. Ordering by value adds only 0.2%. Most plans cost 99–149, so value barely reorders the list. Still the recommended order: it won in all 9 cost scenarios.
  3. LambdaMART lost 22%. It chased the highest-value churners and missed many others (49% reached vs 65%).
  4. Scores still run low. The prediction-day correction moved the expected churn rate from 5.7% to 6.1%; the real rate was 7.3%. For budgets, check last month's outcomes.
Before you rely on it
  1. Fresh outcomes matter. With only outcomes known on the day, the list beats the rule at small budgets (top 5%: 45% vs 30% of churners), not at 10%+.
  2. Scores run low. Members scored ~37% actually left 48% of the time. Recalibrate monthly before using scores in offers.
  3. Auto-renew churners are mostly missed. About 22,000, a third of churners; the list reaches 1 in 4.
  4. Coverage. The test covers 89% of March members, those with a known renewal date.

What P5 delivered
  1. Answers on demand. Four services and an MCP gateway serve apps and AI assistants; a planning agent answers managers' questions in plain words.
  2. Notes that cannot invent facts. Every number and reason is checked against the model's output; if an LLM adds anything, the safe template note is used instead.
  3. Cost and speed known up front. $0.05 per 1,000 AI notes, measured on OpenAI; repeat questions are cached and free; demographic reasons are never quoted to agents.
  4. Forecasts are conservative. The service expects 10.5M a month from the top 10%; March actually delivered 12.4M, because scores still run low.
Questions practitioners ask
Isn't this just "members without auto-renew"?

That is today's rule, and it only ever calls manual renewers. The model reaches 11 points more churners with the same calls, and finds 1 in 4 auto-renew churners the rule never calls.

Did it use information from the future?

No. Every input is built from data dated before the snapshot, 14 days ahead of renewal. An automated test fails if any later record changes a score.

How many members should we call?

Under our central assumptions (1 in 5 saved, 20 per call), profit keeps rising until about 20% of members (≈13.9M a month, vs ≈12.4M at 10%). Try your own save rate and call cost on the "What is the call list worth?" chart: it shows the best share to call for those numbers. Hover the "What is the call list worth?" chart to see the gain for any budget.

Should we order the list by member value?

Yes, but expect little: plans cost 99–149, so ordering by probability × value adds about 0.1%. A learned ranker (LambdaMART) did worse.

Why is this member on the list?

The strongest signals: manual renewal, payment method, a recent cancellation, days since the last payment, and tenure. Per-member reasons come in Phase 5.

Can our CRM or AI assistant use this?

Yes. The scoring and explanation services answer over HTTP, and an MCP gateway exposes four tools (member risk, why at risk, call list, what-if) to any MCP-capable assistant. Every note is checked against the model's facts before it is shown.

Why one AI agent, not several?

The work runs in one line (question → tools → answer), with nothing to do in parallel. Each extra agent adds a 2–3 second round trip and another place to fail, and the "critic" job is already done by a free, deterministic checker. One agent passed 45 of 45 test runs at $0.22 per 1,000 questions. Several agents fit better where independent sources can be read in parallel.

Should we just call the riskiest members?

Not always. Some will leave whatever we offer (Ascarza, JMR 2018). Finding members who respond to an offer is the next project (uplift modelling).

How often does it need refreshing?

Monthly: retrain on the latest outcomes and recalibrate. Drift monitoring is covered in the MLOps project.

What P2 showed
  1. Every week of warning costs churners. Calling 30 days ahead instead of 14 reaches about 3,600 fewer churners a month (60% vs 65%). 7 days reaches about 1,600 more (68%).
  2. 30 days of history is enough. At our 14-day lead, 30 to 180 days of history reach churners within half a point of each other (0.003 PR-AUC), which is run-to-run noise.
  3. Scoring on the renewal date looks best (74%) only because the renewal is already visible. It leaves no time to act.

What P3 showed
  1. Listening habits did not improve the list. The best method added +0.004 PR-AUC against a +0.02 bar. The payment record already carries the signal.
  2. Silent payers (20% of members) pay automatically but have not listened in 60 days. The list reaches only 36% of the ones who leave: a re-engagement message, not a retention call.
  3. Re-engaging members churn most (11%). They are long-time members, a quarter on manual renewal. The list already reaches 82% of them.
5 · Our take

Working view, revised as results come in

So far
  • Framing matters most: lead time and a value-ordered list.
  • Warning time is the real trade-off; history length is not. 30 days of logs is enough.
  • Simple beats clever three times: shape statistics vs encoders (P3), probability × value vs LambdaMART (P4).
  • Drift is real: +2.6 points of churn in one month, so random-split scores would overstate next month.
  • Benchmark scores overstate: a realistic setup loses 0.06 PR-AUC versus training on labels that arrive too late.
  • The open gap is auto-renew cancellers: a third of churners, and the list reaches 1 in 4. That is where Phase 3 behaviour signals have to earn their keep.
  • Benchmark labels can't be fully rebuilt from the shipped data (P0 failed at 98.8%). Official labels stay the test truth.
What would change our mind
  • Answered by P2: lead time is not free (−5 points of reach from 14 to 30 days). We keep 14 days; 7 if campaigns can move faster.
  • Answered by P3: embeddings missed their gate (+0.004 vs +0.02), so they serve personas only. Auto-renew cancellers need a different signal, such as payment-failure history or offer response (CHN-2).

3.1

Churn label rule

In use · P0
  1. Find the member's last expiry from last month's transactions.
  2. A cancellation can only move that expiry earlier.
  3. Renewal within 30 days → retained. Otherwise → churn.
Effective expiryE′ = min(E, Ecancel) Renewal gapg = trenew − E′ Labely = 1 ⟺ no renewal ∨ g ≥ 30

Why it matters: lets us label any month, checked against the official labels (gate ≥ 99%).

3.2

Point-in-time snapshot

In use · P1
  1. Freeze the clock k days before expiry.
  2. Build features from the W days before the freeze.
  3. Anything after the freeze is invisible. A unit test enforces it.
SnapshotS = E − k Feature windowx = f( events with t ∈ [S − W, S) ) Leakage test∀ event used: t < S

Why it matters: the competition predicts at k = 0, which leaves no time to act.

3.3

Observation-window sufficiency

Tested · P2
  1. For each window W: recent 14-day use ÷ window average.
  2. Measure how well that separates churners from stayers (AUC).
  3. Keep the shortest W past the knee.
Decline ratiorW = mean[S−14, S) / mean[S−W, S) SeparationAUC(W) = P( rWstay > rWchurn ) KneeW* = min W with gain ≥ 95% of max

Why it matters: a 2026 study on this dataset found the knee anywhere from 30 to 120 days. Our real data (P2): flat from 30 days, because payment behaviour carries the signal.

3.5

PR-AUC under class imbalance

Decided · ADR-002
  • ROC-AUC: does a churner usually outscore a stayer? Ignores how rare churn is.
  • PR-AUC: how clean is the top of the list? Falls when churn is rare.
Scoreschurn ~ 𝒩(d, 1), stay ~ 𝒩(0, 1) Precision at threshold τP(τ) = π·TPR / (π·TPR + (1−π)·FPR) ROC-AUCΦ(d / √2) — independent of π

Why it matters: the retention team only ever works the top of the list.

Gradient boosting baseline

In use · P1
  1. Start from the average churn rate.
  2. Fit a small tree to what the model still gets wrong.
  3. Add it, shrunk by learning rate η. Repeat.
Additive modelFm(x) = Fm−1(x) + η · hm(x) Pseudo-residual (log-loss)ri = yi − σ(Fm−1(xi)) Leaf value (Newton step)γ = Σri / Σpi(1 − pi)

Why it matters: strongest family on this benchmark, so it is the bar for every deeper idea.

3.8

Profit at k

In use · P4
  • Each call costs c and saves a churner with probability s.
  • Profit = revenue saved − cost of every call.
  • Sort by probability × revenue, not probability alone.
Realised profit of the top kΠ(k) = Σi ∈ top k ( s · yi · ri − c ) Expected value per member𝔼[Πi] = s · p̂i · ri − c

Why it matters: s and c are stated assumptions, varied in a sensitivity table. Real data (P4): probability × value won all 9 cost scenarios, by about 0.1%.

3.9

LambdaMART ranking

Tested · P4 gate failed
  1. For each pair in the wrong order, compute a push λ.
  2. Scale it by the NDCG gain of swapping them.
  3. Trees learn the pushes. Mistakes near the top cost most.
Pair push, for gradei > gradejλij = σ · |ΔNDCGij| / (1 + eσ(si − sj)) Per memberλi = Σj λij − Σj λji Quality, in plain wordsNDCG@5 = how close the top 5 is to the best possible top 5 (0 to 1). Higher tiers count more; rank 1 counts in full, rank 2 63%, rank 3 50%, rank 4 43%, rank 5 39%. Quality, as a formulaNDCG@5 = DCG@5 / IDCG@5,  DCG = Σ (2g − 1) / log₂(rank + 1)

Why it matters: grades are revenue-at-risk tiers. Here λ moves the scores directly so you can watch. Real data (P4): it chased top-tier churners and lost 22% of profit.

Why agents can trust the note
  • Every number in the note must appear in the model's facts.
  • Live test on OpenAI: 60 of 60 notes (48 real members) passed every check before any fallback.
  • The top reason must be named; no other feature may be.
  • Age, gender and city are never quoted to agents.
  • The note ends with one action from a fixed list.
  • If an LLM's text fails any check, the template note is shown instead, and the response says so.
