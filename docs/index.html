3.1

Churn label rule

In use · P0
  1. Find the member's last expiry from last month's transactions.
  2. A cancellation can only move that expiry earlier.
  3. Renewal within 30 days → retained. Otherwise → churn.
Effective expiryE′ = min(E, Ecancel) Renewal gapg = trenew − E′ Labely = 1 ⟺ no renewal ∨ g ≥ 30

Why it matters: lets us label any month, checked against the official labels (gate ≥ 99%).

Simulation · one member, expiry 15 Feb 2017
3.2

Point-in-time snapshot

Decided · ADR-001
  1. Freeze the clock k days before expiry.
  2. Build features from the W days before the freeze.
  3. Anything after the freeze is invisible. A unit test enforces it.
SnapshotS = E − k Feature windowx = f( events with t ∈ [S − W, S) ) Leakage test∀ event used: t < S

Why it matters: the competition predicts at k = 0, which leaves no time to act.

3.3

Observation-window sufficiency

Planned · P2
  1. For each window W: recent 14-day use ÷ window average.
  2. Measure how well that separates churners from stayers (AUC).
  3. Keep the shortest W past the knee.
Decline ratiorW = mean[S−14, S) / mean[S−W, S) SeparationAUC(W) = P( rWstay > rWchurn ) KneeW* = min W with gain ≥ 95% of max

Why it matters: a 2026 study on this dataset found the knee anywhere from 30 to 120 days.

Simulation · 800 synthetic members, half of them churners
3.4

Out-of-time validation

Decided · ADR-002
  1. Train on older monthly cohorts.
  2. Tune on the next month.
  3. Test on the month after.
Out-of-timetrain: c ≤ t · val: c = t+1 · test: c = t+2 Drift in the simulationlogit pm = am + β1,mx1 + β2,mx2,  β1↓ β2↑

Why it matters: churn moved 6.39% → 8.99% in one month. Random splits hide that.

Simulation · 8 monthly cohorts × 1,200 members, logistic model
3.5

PR-AUC under class imbalance

Decided · ADR-002
  • ROC-AUC: does a churner usually outscore a stayer? Ignores how rare churn is.
  • PR-AUC: how clean is the top of the list? Falls when churn is rare.
Scoreschurn ~ 𝒩(d, 1), stay ~ 𝒩(0, 1) Precision at threshold τP(τ) = π·TPR / (π·TPR + (1−π)·FPR) ROC-AUCΦ(d / √2) — independent of π

Why it matters: the retention team only ever works the top of the list.

3.6

Gradient boosting baseline

Planned · P1
  1. Start from the average churn rate.
  2. Fit a small tree to what the model still gets wrong.
  3. Add it, shrunk by learning rate η. Repeat.
Additive modelFm(x) = Fm−1(x) + η · hm(x) Pseudo-residual (log-loss)ri = yi − σ(Fm−1(xi)) Leaf value (Newton step)γ = Σri / Σpi(1 − pi)

Why it matters: strongest family on this benchmark, so it is the bar for every deeper idea.

3.7

Behavioural embeddings

Planned · P3
  1. Hide some days of a member's usage.
  2. Train an encoder to fill them back in.
  3. Its compressed vector becomes features and personas.
Masked reconstructionℒ = Σt ∈ 𝓜 ( xt − x̂t )²,  x̂ = dec(enc(x∖𝓜)) Linear special casea linear auto-encoder with squared loss spans the top principal components

Why it matters: the demo uses the linear case (PCA). The real encoder stays only if it beats RFM by +0.02 PR-AUC.

3.8

Profit at k

Decided · ADR-002
  • Each call costs c and saves a churner with probability s.
  • Profit = revenue saved − cost of every call.
  • Sort by probability × revenue, not probability alone.
Realised profit of the top kΠ(k) = Σi ∈ top k ( s · yi · ri − c ) Expected value per member𝔼[Πi] = s · p̂i · ri − c

Why it matters: s and c are stated assumptions, varied in a sensitivity table.

3.9

LambdaMART ranking

Planned · P4
  1. For each pair in the wrong order, compute a push λ.
  2. Scale it by the NDCG gain of swapping them.
  3. Trees learn the pushes. Mistakes near the top cost most.
Pair push, for gradei > gradejλij = σ · |ΔNDCGij| / (1 + eσ(si − sj)) Per memberλi = Σj λij − Σj λji QualityNDCG@5 = DCG@5 / IDCG@5,  DCG = Σ (2g − 1) / log₂(rank + 1)

Why it matters: grades are revenue-at-risk tiers. Here λ moves the scores directly so you can watch.

5 · Our take

Working view, revised as results come in

So far
  • Framing matters most: lead time and a value-ordered list.
  • Drift is real: +2.6 points of churn in one month, so random-split scores would overstate next month.
  • Benchmark labels can't be fully rebuilt from the shipped data (P0 failed at 98.8%). Official labels stay the test truth.
What would change our mind
  • Lead time costs little (P2) → predict 30 days out, not 14.
  • Embeddings miss their gate (P3) → keep them for personas only.
